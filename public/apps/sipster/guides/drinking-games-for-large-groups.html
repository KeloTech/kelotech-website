Drinking Games for Large Groups (10–20 Players)

Most drinking games break somewhere around player eight: turns take forever, half the room checks their phone, and someone gives up explaining the rules. These games actually scale – up to 20 people.

By the Sipster team · Updated July 2026

Why most drinking games fail with big groups

Card games need a table everyone can reach. Beer pong takes two players at a time while eighteen watch. Rule-heavy games die during the explanation. A big-group game needs three things: everyone involved on every turn, zero equipment bottlenecks, and rules that fit in one sentence.

Games that scale to 10–20 players

1. Never have I ever (whole-room edition)

The gold standard for big groups because every prompt applies to the entire room at once – twenty people can play as fast as five. Grab openers from our question list, or let Sipster deal "for everyone" cards automatically.

2. Most likely to

Read a prompt, count to three, everyone points at someone. Most fingers drinks. It scales beautifully because the voting is the game – and with 15 people the accusations get ruthless.

3. Spin the bottle with a digital wheel

A real bottle in a 20-person circle is physically hopeless. Sipster's spin-the-bottle modes put every player's name on one wheel – the app picks a target fairly, deals them a challenge, and moves on. Nobody argues about where the bottle pointed.

4. Card-draw challenges (Sipster mode)

Draw a card, follow the rule: some cards target one named player, some hit the whole room, some hand out penalties to give away. Because the deck names players itself, the phone just moves around the circle and the game runs at party speed.

How to set up a 20-player game in one minute

  1. One person downloads Sipster – free on the App Store. Nobody else installs anything.
  2. Add up to 20 first names. This is the entire setup.
  3. Pick a mode. For big groups, start with Sipster cards or Party Mode – they mix solo challenges with whole-room cards so the phone never lingers.
  4. Pass the phone clockwise. The app handles whose turn it is, who's targeted, and what the penalty is.

Penalties are sips of whatever each player is drinking – including water or soda – so mixed groups with non-drinkers stay in the game.
