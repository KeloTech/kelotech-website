Guys Night Drinking Games the Boys Will Actually Play

No one's shuffling a deck and no one's reading a rulebook. These guys night drinking games are simple, competitive and appropriately brutal – and the best one lives on a single phone.

By the Sipster team · Updated July 2026

What guys night games need

Three requirements: rules you can explain in one sentence, a way to target your friends specifically, and stakes. If a game can't produce the sentence "that's two penalties, drink up" within five minutes, it's not a guys night game.

The classics

Never have I ever: bros edition

Same rules as always – if you've done it, you drink – but the prompts get progressively more incriminating. This is built into Sipster's Guys Night mode, which deals cards like: "Never have I ever... been so drunk I vomited in a car. If yes – take a penalty." Everyone answers at once, so nobody hides.

Confidence votes

A prompt goes to the group – "who's most likely to get kicked out of the bar tonight?" – and on three, everyone points. Most votes drinks. Sipster deals these automatically so the accusations keep coming.

Challenge cards

Trivia, memory tests and skill challenges with penalties for failure. From Sipster's Party Mode: "Name every country you've visited and give out 1 penalty for each one." Suddenly the guy who did a Europe trip is a weapon.

Guys-only spin the bottle

Sipster's Guys Night mode is a guys-only spin the bottle: every name goes on the wheel, the app picks a victim, and deals a challenge or never-have-I-ever written for the boys. Complete it or take the penalty. No creativity required at 1 a.m. – the app does the thinking.

How to run guys night on one phone

  1. Download Sipster – one guy installs it, everyone plays.
  2. Add the crew. Up to 20 names; every card calls players out by name.
  3. Start with Guys Night mode, then rotate to Sipster cards or Party Mode when you want a gear change.
  4. Penalties, not chaos. A penalty is a sip of whatever you're drinking – beer, whiskey, water. The game works dry too, which matters when someone's driving.
