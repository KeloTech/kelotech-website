Girls Night Party Games: 10 Ideas for an Epic Night In

Bachelorette weekend, birthday, galentines or a random Friday – these girls night games need zero supplies and guarantee at least one story you'll bring up for years.

By the Sipster team · Updated July 2026

What makes a great girls night game?

The best girls night games do three things: they get everyone talking (not staring at a board), they work whether you're four people or fourteen, and they don't require anyone to have bought, printed or prepared anything. Confession-style games rule girls night because the game is really just an excuse for the conversation.

The lineup

1. Girls Night spin the bottle (Sipster)

Sipster's dedicated Girls Night mode is a girls-only spin the bottle: everyone's name goes on the wheel, the app picks a player, and deals a challenge written for a girls night in. Real example from the app: "Sophia, give 2 penalties to the person who takes the most selfies in a day." The group decides who that is – and that's where the screaming starts.

2. Never have I ever

The girls night classic. Someone says something they've never done; everyone who has done it drinks (or drops a finger). Steal openers from our never have I ever questions list, or let Sipster deal them automatically so nobody has to think one up.

3. Truth or drink

Truth or dare's chattier cousin: answer the question honestly or take a sip. Perfect for the "catching up" phase of the night. Our adult truth or dare questions work perfectly here.

4. Most likely to

Someone reads a prompt – "most likely to text their ex tonight" – and on three, everyone points at a person. Most fingers takes the penalty. Sipster deals these as cards in Girls Night and Party Mode, so the prompts keep escalating.

5. Two truths and a lie

Each player tells three stories; the group votes on the lie. Guess wrong, drink. Fool everyone, hand out sips. Zero setup and it works with any group size.

6–10. Rapid-fire ideas

  • Story time roulette – spin to pick who tells an embarrassing story on a theme.
  • Phone roulette – the group picks a (safe) text to send from your phone. High risk, high reward.
  • Cocktail judge – everyone invents a drink; blind taste test; loser's recipe is banned forever.
  • Lip sync battle – losers of any round perform; the group scores it.
  • Would you rather: impossible edition – both options terrible, everyone must choose and defend.

Running the whole night from one phone

You could bounce between listicles, printable cards and half-remembered rules – or open Sipster once:

  1. Add everyone's names (up to 20).
  2. Start with Girls Night mode while everyone settles in.
  3. Switch to Party Mode or 18+ Spin the Bottle as the night escalates – same names, no re-setup.

Every game can be played with drinks or completely alcohol-free, so the designated driver and the pregnant bridesmaid stay in every round.
